What is Unary Notation?

The unary numeral system (base-1) is the simplest numeral system used in mathematics and theoretical computer science. Every positive natural number $k$ is represented by a sequence of $k$ identical tally marks, conventionally using the symbol 1:

  • $1$ is represented as 1
  • $2$ is represented as 11
  • $3$ is represented as 111
  • $5$ is represented as 11111

In theoretical automata analysis, unary notation provides a clean, elegant foundation because arithmetic operations reduce to structural tape operations without the overhead of decimal/binary positional borrow, carry, or base conversion tables.

Input Representation Format

1ᵐ B 1ⁿ

Where 1ᵐ represents the first operand $m$, B acts as the delimiter/separator between the two numbers, and 1ⁿ represents the second operand $n$. Blanks outside the input are represented by the standard automata blank symbol □.

How the Unary Addition Algorithm Works

The addition of $m$ and $n$ on a Turing Machine uses the classic "Bridge & Erase" technique:

  1. Phase 1 (Bridging the Gap): The tape starts with $m$ ones, followed by separator B, followed by $n$ ones. The TM starts in state $q_0$ at the first 1, moves right across operand 1 until it encounters separator B. It replaces B with 1. The tape now contains $(m + 1 + n)$ ones.
  2. Phase 2 (Locating the End): The machine transitions to state $q_1$ and moves right across the second operand's 1s until it hits the right blank boundary □. It then takes one step Left to point directly at the rightmost 1.
  3. Phase 3 (Balancing the Count): In state $q_2$, the machine replaces that rightmost 1 with blank □. This removes the extra 1 introduced in Phase 1, leaving exactly $m + n$ ones on the tape!
  4. Phase 4 (Rewind & Park): In state $q_3$, the head moves left across all the result 1s until it reaches the left blank □. It steps one cell Right to park on the first 1 and halts in state $q_{halt}$.

Complexity Analysis

Time Complexity: $T(n) = O(m + n)$. The head scans right across the entire input length and rewinds once. Total steps $\approx 2(m + n) + 3$.

Space Complexity: $S(n) = O(m + n)$. The computation is in-place and requires no extra auxiliary tape cells.

How Unary Multiplication Works (Nested Iteration)

Multiplication $m \times n$ is computed as repeated addition (adding $n$ to an accumulator $m$ times):

  1. Setup: The machine scans past $1^m B 1^n$ and places a boundary marker B to mark the product accumulator region: 1ᵐ B 1ⁿ B □.
  2. Outer Loop (Multiplicand): For each 1 in the first operand, the TM marks it as X and crosses over to the second operand.
  3. Inner Loop (Multiplier Replication): For each 1 in the second operand, the TM marks it as Y, travels past the second separator to the product zone, and appends a 1 to the accumulator.
  4. Multiplier Reset: Once all 1s of the second operand are copied for the current X, the machine rewinds and converts all Y marks back to 1, then returns to the first operand.
  5. Cleanup: Once all 1s of operand 1 are marked X, the TM erases the input operands and separators, parking cleanly on the final product 1ᵐˣⁿ!

Complexity Analysis

Time Complexity: $O(m \cdot n^2)$ due to head travel back and forth across the tape during replication.

Space Complexity: $O(m + n + m \cdot n)$ to store the multiplicand, multiplier, and product accumulator.
